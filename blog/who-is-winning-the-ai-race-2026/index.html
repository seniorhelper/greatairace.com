Who is winning the AI race in 2026?

By the Great AI Race desk · Nov 17, 2026 · 6 min read

"Who is winning the AI race?" sounds like a simple question. It is not. A lab can lead on users and trail on benchmarks, or own the biggest data center and still release fewer models. The Great AI Race answers it the only fair way we know: six lanes of sourced data, each scored 0 to 100 against the other labs, then averaged into one composite score. As of Nov 17, 2026, with every lane weighted equally, here is how the field stands.

About these numbers. Every figure below comes from the public source linked next to it and carries the date that source covers. Where a lab does not publish a number, we say so rather than estimate it. Scores come from the Great AI Race methodology as of Nov 17, 2026.

The short answer

With equal weights, OpenAI leads with a composite of 80.5. Google DeepMind is second at 73.9, followed by Meta at 65.7 and Anthropic at 65.2. xAI sits fifth at 55.3. Behind them come Alibaba's Qwen team (47.6), DeepSeek (42.3), Moonshot AI (38.8) and Mistral AI (27.4).

Why OpenAI leads: reach

OpenAI's lead comes mostly from the Reach lane, where it scores a full 100. ChatGPT drew 55.7% of worldwide generative AI website traffic in October 2026, according to Similarweb data reported by OfficeChai. Reports based on internal figures put ChatGPT at about 1 billion weekly active users. OpenAI also scores well on Money: a new round reported by Bloomberg values it at $1.25 trillion, and the Financial Times reported about $50 billion in annualized revenue at the end of the September quarter.

Where OpenAI gives ground is Openness. Its gpt-oss open-weight models date from August 2025, and we could not re-verify a newer open release, so it receives the base 50 points in that lane.

Google DeepMind: the biggest balance sheet and a strong model lineup

Google DeepMind's parent, Alphabet, has the largest market cap in the field at about $3.64 trillion (Nov 13, 2026 close), which tops the Money lane. Google reported that the Gemini app passed 1 billion monthly active users in its Q3 2026 earnings remarks. On Nov 10 it made Gemini 4 Carbon generally available, and Carbon scored 60 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index after the v5.0.1 revision, as reported by OfficeChai.

Google's weakest lane is Muscle. That lane uses Epoch AI's estimate of each lab's largest single data center, and Google spreads its TPU fleet across many sites, so a single-site measure undercounts it. We note that limit on the methodology page.

Anthropic: top benchmark score, biggest single site

Anthropic climbed to the top of the Brains lane this month. Claude Fable 5.2, released Nov 9, 2026, scored 65 at max effort on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, five points ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon, according to OfficeChai's report on the results. Anthropic also tops Muscle: Epoch AI lists its New Carlisle, Indiana site with Amazon at about 1,037 MW of current IT power, the largest on Epoch's list. And it leads Momentum with seven major releases in the last six months.

Two things hold it back. Anthropic has not published Claude user counts, so its Reach score rests only on web traffic share (11.5% in October). And it has no open-weight models, so it scores 0 in Openness. Its pending IPO could also move the Money lane: an updated prospectus reported a run rate of roughly $81 billion as of Oct 31.

Meta: open weights and a giant parent

Meta lands third on the strength of Money (Meta's market cap was about $1.64 trillion on Nov 13) and Openness. Its Muse Spark Open weights, released in mid-October, are among the most recent open releases from a frontier lab, per OrcaRouter. Meta does not disclose a Meta AI monthly user count, so its Reach lane scores 0 under our rules, which costs it a lot of ground.

The rest of the field

xAI is strong on compute. Epoch AI estimates Colossus 2 at about 946 MW of current IT power. Its Money figure is SpaceX's total market cap, which includes far more than AI. Alibaba's Qwen team keeps shipping. Its Qwen4-Max preview opened on Nov 11. DeepSeek and Moonshot AI score high in Openness. Moonshot posted the Kimi K3.1 Pro weights on Hugging Face on Nov 5. Mistral AI is the smallest lab on the track by valuation, but it released Mistral Large 4 weights in late October.

The track also has a tenth racer that is not ranked: Big AI Brain, a home-grown AI built from scratch with no outside LLM. It runs in its own exhibition lane, dead last for now.

What could change the order next

  • Anthropic's IPO. Anthropic set an IPO range of $70 to $78 per share, which implies about $1.2 trillion at the midpoint. We keep the $965 billion Series H figure until the IPO prices, then switch to the market cap.
  • Benchmark updates. Artificial Analysis revises its index as new models arrive. Grok 4.8 and Qwen4-Max have not been scored yet.
  • User disclosures. If Anthropic or Meta publish user counts, their Reach scores would rise sharply, because a missing figure scores zero today.

How to read the race

The composite is a gauge, not a prophecy. It rewards labs that publish their numbers, and it cannot see private deals, internal models or anything a lab keeps to itself. That is why every score on this site links to its inputs and every input links to its source. If you want to compare the top three labs in depth, read OpenAI vs Google DeepMind vs Anthropic. FAQ

Who is winning the AI race right now?

Which AI lab has the best model in November 2026?

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index after the v5.0.1 revision, Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 scored 65 at max effort, ahead of Google's Gemini 4 Carbon at 60 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra at 57, according to OfficeChai's report on the results.

Why does a lab score zero in a lane?

If a lab does not publish any figure for a lane, such as user counts, that lane scores zero. The Great AI Race never estimates a missing number.
