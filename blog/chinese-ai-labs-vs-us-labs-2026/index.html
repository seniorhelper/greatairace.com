Chinese AI labs vs US labs in 2026: where each side leads

By the Great AI Race desk · Nov 17, 2026 · 6 min read

Ask whether China is catching up in AI and you will get confident answers in both directions. Both can be partly right, because they measure different things. This article looks at the three Chinese labs on our track, Alibaba's Qwen team, DeepSeek and Moonshot AI, next to the US labs, lane by lane, as of Nov 17, 2026.

About these numbers. Every figure below comes from the public source linked next to it and carries the date that source covers. Where a figure could not be verified, we leave it out rather than estimate it. This page was checked on Nov 17, 2026.

The short answer

US labs lead on the top benchmark scores, on compute and on money. Chinese labs lead on open-weight models, which developers around the world download and build on. How large the model gap is depends on which yardstick you use, and the yardsticks currently disagree.

Benchmarks: close or far, depending on the test

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0.1, the top US models scored 65 (Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2), 60 (Google's Gemini 4 Carbon) and 57 (OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra), as reported by OfficeChai. The highest Chinese model on the v5 board was Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro at 46, ahead of Qwen3.8 Max at 34 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at 32.

Other measures tell a similar but not identical story. Epoch AI's Capabilities Index, which combines dozens of benchmarks, had GPT-6 Astra at 168.9 and Kimi K3 at 158.7 in late October. OfficeChai noted that this gap of about 10 points was among the widest since 2023, after it had narrowed to single digits at times in 2023 to 2025.

Human-preference rankings look much closer. Stanford's 2026 AI Index, published in April, found the top US model on the Arena leaderboard ahead of the top Chinese model by 39 Elo points, or 2.7%. Arena rankings reward answers people prefer in head-to-head chats, which is a different skill from hard reasoning tests. Timing also matters. A single release can move any of these numbers, and Chinese labs have shipped often in 2026.

Some Chinese researchers caution against reading too much into public scores. In January, Zhipu AI co-founder Tang Jie said the gap may in fact be widening, because benchmarks only capture models that have been released.

Open models: China's strongest lane

Chinese labs dominate open-weight AI. In a September briefing to members of Congress and their staff, researcher Nathan Lambert said Chinese models had grown to over 80% of open-model usage on OpenRouter, and that Chinese models had about 3.2 billion Hugging Face downloads, twice the US total. All three Chinese labs on our track publish open weights, and Moonshot posted Kimi K3.1 Pro weights on Nov 5. Among US labs, Google (Gemma 4) and Meta (Muse Spark Open) also release open models, while Anthropic does not. For the trade-offs, see open-weight vs closed AI models.

Compute: the widest gap

Compute is where the gap is largest. Using Epoch AI's chip import data, OfficeChai reported that the US accounted for 72.2% of AI compute added since 2022, against 9.6% for China, and for 78% of new compute in the first half of 2026. Al Jazeera, also citing Epoch, put the US at nearly three-quarters of the world's AI computing power and China at just over 14%. The figures differ because they count different things, cumulative purchases versus installed stock, but both show a large US lead.

Export controls are a big reason. The US restricts sales of the most advanced chips to China, and China does not yet make its own extreme ultraviolet lithography machines, eWeek reported. Alibaba Qwen's technical lead Lin Junyang said US labs have one to two orders of magnitude more computing resources than their Chinese counterparts. Chinese labs have answered partly by focusing on efficiency, which is one reason their open models punch above their compute. For the full compute picture, read the AI compute race.

Money: a large gap, with some public listings

US labs and their parents are far larger by valuation. OpenAI's reported round is at $1.25 trillion. DeepSeek's latest round valued it at $50 billion, the highest of any Chinese AI startup per OfficeChai, and Moonshot's at $35 billion. Spending follows the same pattern. Goldman Sachs estimated about $764 billion of 2026 capital spending by the five US hyperscalers, while Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu and ByteDance were expected to spend about $102 billion. Chinese hyperscaler spending, however, was projected to grow faster in 2026. China also produced the first stock market listings of pure model makers, Zhipu AI and MiniMax in Hong Kong. Our AI lab IPOs explainer covers both.

Talent and research

China publishes more AI research by volume. Al Jazeera, citing the Center for Security and Emerging Technology, reported that China produced more than 27% of global AI publications in 2024 against 12% for the US, counting only papers with English titles or abstracts. Talent flows both ways: MacroPolo data from 2022, cited in the same piece, found that 72% of top AI researchers educated in China were working in the US.

What to watch next

  • New releases. Each new frontier model from either side resets the benchmark gap. Watch the Brains lane after every major launch.
  • Chip access. Any change in US export rules, or progress in Chinese chipmaking, would move the compute picture more than any single model.
  • Open-weight licensing. Licenses shape how widely open models spread. A shift in licensing or release habits on either side would show up in the Openness lane.
  • Disclosure. As more labs list on stock exchanges, more of their finances and capacity become public, which fills in blanks on our fact cards.

How this shows up on the track

On the Great AI Race, the three Chinese labs rank sixth to eighth with equal weights. Each scores between 87 and 98 in Openness, trails the US leaders in Money, and scores 0 in Muscle because no current capacity figure could be verified for them. Moonshot's Brains score of 71 is the closest any of the three comes to the US leaders. Turn up the Openness slider on the race track and they move up quickly. Turn up Muscle and the gap widens. Neither view is the "right" one. They answer different questions about the race.

FAQ

Is China ahead of the US in AI?

Not overall as of Nov 17, 2026. US labs hold the top benchmark scores, most of the world's AI compute and far larger valuations. Chinese labs lead in open-weight models, with over 80% of open-model usage on OpenRouter according to Nathan Lambert's September 2026 briefing.

What is the best Chinese AI model in November 2026?

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5, Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro scored 46, the highest of the Chinese models we track, ahead of Qwen3.8 Max at 34 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at 32.

How big is the gap between US and Chinese AI models?

It depends on the measure. Stanford's 2026 AI Index found a 2.7% gap on the Arena leaderboard in April, while Epoch AI's Capabilities Index showed a gap of about 10 points in late October, among the widest since 2023.

Why do Chinese labs have less compute?

US export controls restrict sales of the most advanced AI chips to China, and China does not yet make its own EUV lithography machines. Epoch AI chip import data shows China with about 9.6% of AI compute added since 2022, against 72.2% for the US.

Which Chinese AI labs are on the Great AI Race?

Alibaba's Qwen team, DeepSeek and Moonshot AI. Each has its own fact card with sourced figures on the racers page.
