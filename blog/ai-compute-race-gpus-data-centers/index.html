The AI compute race: who has the most chips and data centers?

By the Great AI Race desk · Nov 17, 2026 · 6 min read

Every frontier model is trained and served on enormous amounts of computing power. That makes compute one of the clearest signs of who can keep up in the AI race. It is also one of the hardest things to measure, because labs rarely publish chip counts and much of their capacity is rented. This guide explains the three ways compute is usually measured and who leads on each, as of Nov 17, 2026.

About these numbers. Every figure below comes from the public source linked next to it and carries the date that source covers. Where a figure could not be verified, we leave it out rather than estimate it. This page was checked on Nov 17, 2026.

Three ways to measure compute

  • Chips, in H100-equivalents. Researchers convert every AI chip, whether an NVIDIA GPU or a Google TPU, into the computing power of one NVIDIA H100. This is called H100e. It is the closest thing to a head count of compute.
  • Power, in megawatts or gigawatts. Data centers are often sized by how much electricity their IT equipment can draw. It is easier to observe from outside, but a megawatt of newer chips does much more work than a megawatt of older ones.
  • Money, in capital spending and contracts. Companies report what they spend on buildings and hardware, and labs announce multi-year compute deals. These show where capacity is heading, not what is running today.

The three measures often rank companies differently. Keep that in mind whenever you see a headline about who has the most.

By chips owned: the cloud giants lead

Epoch AI, a research group that tracks AI hardware, estimates that five hyperscalers, Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle, held about 71% of the world's cumulative AI compute at the end of 2025, up from 63% in early 2024. Google was the single largest owner, with about a quarter of global capacity, or roughly 5 million H100e, mostly on its own TPU chips rather than NVIDIA GPUs. Microsoft, Amazon and Meta rounded out the top four.

The frontier labs themselves own or use less than you might think. In a May 2026 analysis, Epoch put the world's operational AI compute at about 16 million H100e at the end of 2025, and estimated OpenAI at about 1.7 million H100e and Anthropic at over 1 million. OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI combined were probably under 4 million H100e, or about 20 to 30% of the world total. Google and Meta together owned about a third of world AI compute, with Google DeepMind using slightly under half of Google's share. Epoch called some of these figures rough first-pass estimates.

Why the gap between owning and using? Labs like OpenAI and Anthropic rent most of their capacity from cloud providers, so the chips sit on someone else's books.

By single site: the biggest AI data centers

Epoch also tracks the largest individual AI data centers by power. On its list as of Nov 6, 2026, the biggest by current IT power was the Anthropic and Amazon site in New Carlisle, Indiana, at about 1,037 MW. xAI's Colossus 2 in Memphis followed at about 946 MW, and OpenAI's Stargate site in Abilene, Texas at about 824 MW. Meta's Prometheus site in New Albany, Ohio was at about 631 MW, with a 1 GW target, per OfficeChai's late-October roundup of Epoch data.

By company totals and commitments

Some labs report fleet-wide totals. SpaceX said xAI had 1.7 GW of nameplate compute at the end of Q3 2026, up from 1.4 GW in June. OpenAI said in April that it had secured 10 GW of US compute capacity, which means contracted, not necessarily online. Epoch noted that OpenAI tripled its data center power capacity in both 2024 and 2025.

Spending shows where the next wave is coming from. Alphabet guided to $195 billion to $205 billion of capital spending in 2026. Goldman Sachs estimated the five US hyperscalers would spend about $764 billion in 2026, as reported by Al Jazeera. Alibaba, home of Qwen, reported $9.975 billion of capital spending in the June 2026 quarter, up 75% from a year earlier.

What about the smaller labs?

DeepSeek, Moonshot AI and Mistral AI do not publish current capacity figures we could verify, so under our missing-data rule they score 0 in the Muscle lane. That is a gap in disclosure, not proof they have no compute. Chinese labs also face US export controls on the most advanced chips. Our Chinese vs US labs article covers that side of the story.

How to read a compute headline

Compute stories move fast, and the numbers in them are easy to misread. Before you compare two claims, ask four questions:

  • Is it owned or used? A cloud provider can own chips that a lab rents. Counting both double-counts the same hardware.
  • Is it online or planned? A gigawatt "secured" or "announced" may take years to switch on. Current IT power is what runs models today.
  • Is it one site or the whole fleet? A single campus can be the biggest in the world while its owner's total fleet is smaller than a rival's spread-out network.
  • What date does it cover? Capacity is growing so fast that a figure from six months ago can be badly out of date. Every number in this article carries the date its source covers.

Asked this way, the apparent contradictions mostly disappear. Google can own the most chips, Anthropic can run the biggest single site, and OpenAI can point to 10 GW under contract, all at the same time.

Does more compute mean winning?

Not on its own. Compute is an input, not a result. A lab with fewer chips can still ship strong models by using them efficiently, and open-weight labs have shown how far efficient training can go. But the frontier is expensive. Epoch estimated that the world's AI compute stock roughly tripled in 2025, and the leading labs are still racing to build bigger sites. FAQ

Which company has the most AI compute?

Google. Epoch AI estimated that Google held about a quarter of the world's AI compute at the end of 2025, roughly 5 million H100-equivalents, mostly on its own TPU chips.

What is an H100-equivalent?

It is a unit that converts any AI chip into the computing power of one NVIDIA H100 GPU, so fleets built from different chips can be compared.

What is the largest AI data center in 2026?

On Epoch AI's list as of Nov 6, 2026, the largest by current IT power was the Anthropic and Amazon site in New Carlisle, Indiana, at about 1,037 MW, followed by xAI's Colossus 2 at about 946 MW.

Do OpenAI and Anthropic own their own chips?

Mostly not. Epoch AI notes that OpenAI and Anthropic largely depend on cloud providers such as Microsoft, Amazon and Google for their compute, so much of the hardware they use is owned by those companies.

How does the Great AI Race score compute?

The Muscle lane uses Epoch AI's estimate of each lab's largest single data center by current IT power. It understates labs with spread-out fleets, such as Google, which is noted on the methodology page.
