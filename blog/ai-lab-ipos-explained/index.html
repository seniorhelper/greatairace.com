AI lab IPOs explained: who is public and who is going public

By the Great AI Race desk · Nov 17, 2026 · 6 min read

For most of the AI boom, the labs building frontier models were private. You could not buy a share of OpenAI or Anthropic on a stock exchange, and the closest public option was a big tech parent like Alphabet or Meta. That is changing in 2026. Two Chinese model makers listed in Hong Kong in January, SpaceX listed in June after absorbing xAI, and Anthropic is marketing its IPO right now. Here is who is public, who is on the way, and who is still private, as of Nov 17, 2026.

How an AI lab goes public, in plain English

A US IPO usually follows the same path. The company files a registration statement, called an S-1, with the Securities and Exchange Commission. It can file a confidential draft first, so the SEC can comment before anything is public. Later it files publicly, which reveals its finances. Next comes the roadshow, when bankers pitch the shares to big investors at a price range. On pricing day the final price is set. Trading starts soon after, and insiders usually face a lock-up period before they can sell.

Two numbers get mixed up a lot. The offering size is how much money the IPO raises. The valuation or market cap is what the whole company is worth at the IPO price. A company can raise $60 billion and be valued at more than a trillion dollars at the same time.

Already public: the big parents

Three labs on our track sit inside companies that were public long before the AI race. Google DeepMind is part of Alphabet, worth about $3.64 trillion at the Nov 13, 2026 close. Meta was worth about $1.64 trillion on the same date. Alibaba, home of the Qwen models, trades in New York and Hong Kong (market data). Owning these stocks gives you exposure to much more than AI: search, ads, social apps, cloud and e-commerce. None of the three reports its AI lab as a separate business, which is why our Money lane flags parent-company figures.

SpaceX and xAI: a lab inside a rocket company

xAI took an unusual route. SpaceX acquired it earlier in 2026, so xAI is now part of SpaceX. SpaceX then priced the largest IPO on record: 555,555,555 shares at $135 each, raising $75 billion, on June 11, 2026, under the ticker SPCX. At the IPO price that valued SpaceX at about $1.75 trillion. As with Alphabet and Meta, a SpaceX share covers far more than AI. Rockets and the Starlink satellite network sit alongside the Grok models.

Hong Kong got there first: Zhipu and MiniMax

The first companies built around large language models to list anywhere were Chinese. Zhipu AI began trading in Hong Kong on Jan 8, 2026, raising more than HKD 4.3 billion (about US$552 million) and closing its first day up 12.4%. Yicai called it the world's first large language model developer to go public. MiniMax followed the next day, and Yicai reported its stock doubled in value on debut. Neither company is one of the nine racers on our track, but their listings set the first public price tags on pure model makers.

Anthropic: the big one in progress

Anthropic made its S-1 public on Oct 26, 2026, for a listing on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT, according to FutureSearch's IPO tracker. It had filed a confidential draft on June 1. On Nov 10 it launched its roadshow with a price range of $70 to $78 a share. The offering covers 801.35 million new Class A shares plus 23.65 million shares from selling stockholders, and underwriters have an option on up to 123.75 million more. Alongside the IPO, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC Partners agreed to buy about $26 billion of a separate class of stock at the IPO price.

What does that make Anthropic worth? It depends on the share count used. Renaissance Capital put the midpoint at about $1.2 trillion, while The Next Web reported up to $1.4 trillion fully diluted at the top of the range. Its last private round, the Series H, valued it at $965 billion. Our Money lane keeps the Series H figure until shares actually trade.

The filing also shows the cost of the race. Anthropic reported $17 billion of revenue for the quarter ended Sep 30, 2026, and a net loss of $104 billion for the first nine months of the year, per RTTNews. As of our Nov 17 check, the IPO had not priced. Reports have pointed to a debut before Thanksgiving, but dates can move.

OpenAI: filed quietly, not this year

OpenAI filed a confidential S-1 on June 8, 2026, FutureSearch reports. In August its CFO told employees OpenAI would be a public company in 2027 if not sooner, and in September Sam Altman ruled out a 2026 IPO (FutureSearch summary). For now it raises money privately. A new round reported by Bloomberg values it at $1.25 trillion. Being private has not slowed it down on our track: OpenAI leads the composite on the strength of ChatGPT's reach.

Still private: DeepSeek, Moonshot and Mistral

The three smallest labs on the track by valuation are private, and we found no public IPO filing for any of them. DeepSeek raised $7.4 billion at a $50 billion valuation, which OfficeChai described as the highest of any Chinese AI startup. Moonshot AI, maker of Kimi, raised $3.5 billion at a $35 billion valuation. Mistral AI closed a $3 billion Series D at a $21 billion post-money valuation in September. Private labs do not have to publish quarterly results, which is one reason their fact cards show more "not disclosed" entries.

Not every AI IPO is going ahead

The window is not wide open for everyone. In late October, Reuters reported (via Khaleej Times) that several AI infrastructure listings had been pushed back or paused, including Nscale, Switch and SB Energy, while Anthropic stood mostly alone among big AI offerings. Market mood can change fast, and so can IPO calendars.

Why IPOs matter for the race

A listing does two things that matter for this scoreboard. It raises cash that can go straight into compute, which feeds the Muscle lane. And it brings regular, audited disclosure, which turns guesses into facts. When Anthropic starts trading, its Money figure will switch from a private round to a live market cap. FAQ

Is Anthropic a public company?

Not yet as of Nov 17, 2026. Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow on Nov 10, 2026 with a price range of $70 to $78 a share for a proposed Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT. The IPO had not priced at the time of our check.

When will OpenAI go public?

OpenAI has not set a date. It filed a confidential S-1 in June 2026, its CFO told employees it would be public in 2027 if not sooner, and Sam Altman ruled out a 2026 IPO, according to FutureSearch's summary of reports.

Can you buy xAI stock?

Not on its own. SpaceX acquired xAI in 2026 and listed on June 12, 2026 under the ticker SPCX, so xAI is part of SpaceX rather than a separately traded company.

Which AI model company went public first?

Zhipu AI, which began trading in Hong Kong on Jan 8, 2026, was described by Yicai as the world's first large language model developer to go public. MiniMax listed in Hong Kong the next day.
